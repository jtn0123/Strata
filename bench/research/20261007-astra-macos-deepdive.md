# Strata on our 48 GiB M5 Pro: source-level follow-up

Research snapshot: October 7, 2026. Read-only GitHub/source inspection; no builds, model loads, benchmarks, installations or runtime changes. Existing dirty work was preserved. No VM/build work was stopped, and no Colima/Grafana action was taken. Evidence is under `bench/runtime/upstream-review/20261007-astra/`, including the companion review's `root-pr-checks/` diffs.

The useful conclusion is narrower than the first review suggested: **we already have several of the attractive optimizations, and the native Mac port's best-known state optimization fixes duplication that our llama.cpp graph does not have.** There are worthwhile correctness backports and better measurement/fit methods. There is no newly identified patch with a defensible promised TPS gain on our current configuration.

Our comparison target remains full Qwen3.8-Flash-Next Q2_0, 512 experts/10 used, short MTP (three or four drafts), all 49/49 layers on GPU, mmap/lazy table, one slot and `-fit off`. Historical 47.61/48.91 TPS synthetic results and workload-dependent 38.99–54.19 TPS are historical controls, not fresh results. The dense 4B diagnostic smoke does not establish full-model route costs; full Flash diagnostics and completion of the two-launch baseline refresh remain held.

## What changed in this review

1. **Native GDN optimization: demote from proposed import to architectural reference.** Its `Verifier::capture()` writes recurrence state in place only for `T == 1`, all experts resident, with additional stage guards. Our `src/models/delta-net-base.cpp:527` already computes output and new state once; the rollback path produces retained snapshots and copies them into recurrent cache. We have no matching unconditional second recurrence to remove. Any opportunity is in measured snapshot/copy/checkpoint traffic, not the advertised duplicate-recurrence fix. [Native verifier, lines 1152–1173](https://github.com/liangxiwei/Strata-for-mac/blob/56075e03265f7be44e65d6c9de0574eaa0b7e13d/src/core/verify.cpp#L1152).
2. **Native-port benchmark history needs a correctness timeline.** Old Q2_0 100K and MTP results used an implementation with a Q3_K dequantization error. Later corrected 10K and October 4 128K results must be distinguished from those historical runs. A failed old retrieval test is not evidence that Q2_0 itself is unsuitable. [Correction and parity report](https://github.com/liangxiwei/Strata-for-mac/blob/56075e03265f7be44e65d6c9de0574eaa0b7e13d/bench/results/2026-10-03-metal-iq2-xs/README.md).
3. **Meld's full hyper-connection fusion is genuinely absent, but its own patch reports no useful speedup:** pp1 −2.7%, pp4 +0.6%. Our graph already has a smaller sigmoid/stream-mean fusion. Counting “12 operations replaced by three” as a likely gain would be misleading. [Patch 0005](https://github.com/MeldlabsAI/meld-turbo/blob/ef2101f5f2517204e1d755522d0b84346c4c9f0a/patches/0005-ggml-fused-hyper-connection-read-GGML_OP_HC_MIX-CPU-.patch).
4. **Previously omitted llama.cpp work includes two useful correctness candidates**, not just new kernels: #30100 and, before any quantized-KV experiment, #29340. The qwen4exp graph-reallocation fix #29958 is already incorporated locally.
5. **Memory-fit heuristics are not an MTP admission controller.** metalfit's fixed 1.2 GiB compute reserve and 4.3 GiB system-wired allowance are measured on another setup; its planner has no explicit MTP-helper, rollback-snapshot or QSA-indexer budget. Reuse its accounting structure, not its numerical safety conclusion.

## Forks worth studying, with source compatibility

### liangxiwei/Strata-for-mac: strongest native-engine reference

Fresh branch inventory: `metal-mac` and `codex/metal-canonical-expert-down` both point to **56075e03265f7be44e65d6c9de0574eaa0b7e13d**. `main` is **99f3dbd0b21d1401b3769e0c0d963913607f380b**; merely looking at main misses the work.

The resident path captures a complete verification window only when experts are all resident; otherwise it records segments for host participation. This illustrates why avoiding CPU routing round trips matters. However, our llama.cpp already has graph reuse (`llama-context.cpp:1414`) and GPU expert operations. “One graph” does not by itself show a missing feature or an avoidable synchronization in our engine.

The latest expert specialization is precise: `native_expert_resident()` stages gate/up, SwiGLU and quantized activations, then dispatches a direct Q2_0 down kernel; the canonical variant applies only to embedding 2560 and expert FF 640. This is worth studying as a shape-specific implementation, not cherry-picking as a generic ggml kernel. [Exact launcher](https://github.com/liangxiwei/Strata-for-mac/blob/56075e03265f7be44e65d6c9de0574eaa0b7e13d/src/kernels/metal/iq_kernels.mm#L370).

Its M2 Max/96 GB/IQ2_XS, MTP-off GDN comparison improved warm median 21.78→22.55 TPS. The experiment's commit graph dropped from 134 to 98 entries. These figures concern its own duplicate work, not ours. Its IQ2_XS numerical audit is stronger evidence than a readable answer: fixed inputs, logits and committed states across short, 10K and cached continuations. Still, finite sample equivalence is not universal quality proof. [GDN experiment](https://github.com/liangxiwei/Strata-for-mac/blob/56075e03265f7be44e65d6c9de0574eaa0b7e13d/bench/results/2026-10-03-metal-gdn-inplace/README.md), [model audit](https://github.com/liangxiwei/Strata-for-mac/blob/56075e03265f7be44e65d6c9de0574eaa0b7e13d/bench/results/2026-10-03-metal-iq2-xs-opt/README.md).

Each model had one prompt; raw run output remained on the author's machine. Q2_0 sampled physical footprint was 41.9G on a 96 GB host. This does not establish 128K reliability, memory admission or speed on our 48 GiB system. Power-mode comparisons also disclose changed prompts/background-load gaps. [Corrected 128K report](https://github.com/liangxiwei/Strata-for-mac/blob/56075e03265f7be44e65d6c9de0574eaa0b7e13d/bench/results/2026-10-04-metal-128k/README.md).

### MeldlabsAI/meld-turbo: useful negative results and compatible patch experiments

Current **ef2101f5f2517204e1d755522d0b84346c4c9f0a**. Our Q2 masked kernel and draft-vocabulary work already draw on this project; do not count them again. Patch 0005 adds `GGML_OP_HC_MIX`, CPU reference, three Metal stages and an opt-in qwen4exp path for 1–8 tokens, float-family projection weights and F32 norms. Our target batch range fits, but its own end-to-end result does not support adoption. This is a good counterexample to assuming fewer launches are always faster: it still reads substantial projection weights.

Its larger published speed story combines MTP, a smaller draft vocabulary and dense Q8 re-encoding. That changes weights and memory footprint; the published 64 GB result is a simulation on a 96 GB machine. Our M5 matrix path changes the old comparison again. ### vamzi/tierpack: useful evidence against optimistic paging claims

Current **bd397562c0dc7661f031747cc5d6a9e3e48e2c06**. Lossless packing changes which tensors share mapped ranges; it does not reduce the information content of the weights. On an M3 Max/48 GB, a larger hybrid model's repeated-token synthetic test gave around 20 TPS while real text fell to roughly 0.2–1.8 TPS. That demonstrates expert-locality bias, not a universal ratio between synthetic and real performance. Our fully resident controls remain useful; paging experiments need actual text and route diversity. [Implementation](https://github.com/vamzi/tierpack/blob/bd397562c0dc/tierpack.py), [measurement disclosures](https://github.com/vamzi/tierpack/blob/bd397562c0dc/docs/BENCHMARKS.md).

## Held validation plan

Do not automatically start any step. Preserve the no-full-model-run hold, current resource gates and unrelated work.

1. Complete the fresh baseline only when released: same binary/model/helper/prompt hashes, three/four-draft arms kept distinct, fixed sampler and output cap, at least two independent launches. Report prompt TPS, user-visible TTFT, decode TPS and whole-response time; record thermal/clock/background conditions, pressure and swap deltas.
2. Capture full-model routes and split operation costs without mixing instrumentation timing into baseline history. Determine whether shared matrices, routed matvecs, state copies/checkpoints or lazy-table stalls dominate. Dense smoke is insufficient.
3. One change per arm. Correctness backport first gets its minimal reproducer and focused backend cases. Kernel/state candidates get same-input logits/tokens plus full/partial acceptance, rejection, rollback, cached continuation and cancellation/state reset checks. A speedup with unexplained state divergence is rejected.
4. Compute a measured ceiling before coding: if a candidate accounts for fraction `f` of wall time, even removing it entirely caps speedup at `1/(1-f)`. No fixed percentage is defensible before the profile.
5. Use interleaved controls and identical uninstrumented settings; reject trials with new swap, substantial pressure changes or unrelated workload interference. Report median and run spread, acceptance and generated-token count. Keep optional warmup, cache precision, context length and weight re-encoding separate.
6. For future paging/prefetch: real text across code, English and Chinese plus copy/edit tasks; record expert reuse/misses and file-specific bytes/wait per generated token. Bound cache bytes, cancellation and in-flight requests; compare useful latency saved against added RAM and I/O. No whole-table lock, larger-model download or raised system memory limit is justified by this research.

Scope: fresh metadata/branch checks on selected relevant projects, source inspection of native Metal hot paths and benchmark artifacts, five specific PC-branch commit diffs, and local overlap checks including the companion reviewer’s additional llama.cpp PR inspection. This is not an exhaustive audit of every public fork/branch. Two initial shortened repository-name probes returned 404; corrected names were refreshed successfully. No missing-repository inference is drawn from those failures.
